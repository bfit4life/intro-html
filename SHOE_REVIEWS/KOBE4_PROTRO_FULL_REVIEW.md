## ARCHETYPE MATCH

### Shifty Guard (140-185 lbs) — STURDY
This is the shoe's home address. Fast decel, lateral explosion, court feel as competitive advantage — the Kobe 4 Protro delivers every single one of those things at the highest available level. If you're a shifty guard who has sized correctly and trusts your ankles, this is one of the best competition shoes on the planet. The traction and court feel combination is unmatched in the modern market.

### Explosive Wing (185-225 lbs) — STURDY
Wings who play perimeter-out will find this shoe lives up to the hype. The torsion system handles the loads of a wing cutting through traffic. The low-cut demands ankle stability — if you have it, you're rewarded with movement freedom that high-cut shoes can't offer. Wings between 215-225 who play with interior physicality should monitor cushion feedback over extended sessions.

### Heavy Forward (215-270+ lbs) — NOT IT
The Kobe 4 Protro is a precision instrument. Heavy forwards are playing a different sport in a different body. The thin Zoom Air is not a negotiation — at these weights, on repeated post footwork and landing impact, it will bottom out. The containment can't compensate for what the cushion can't absorb. Respect the tool. Find the right one.

### Older Hooper — SHAKY
The attraction is real — legendary traction and court feel make you feel like you have your whole game. The problem is what the thin Zoom does to aging joints over 40 minutes. Knees and ankles that need cushion buffering won't get enough from the Kobe 4 stack. Short sessions on clean courts, lighter older players — possible. Full competitive games with aging joints — the risk is real.

### Volleyball-Style Mover — SHAKY
Forefoot responsiveness is excellent. The low profile is ideal for a vertical-game athlete. But the cushion on landing is where this falls short. Volleyball-style loads on repeated jump landings without adequate cushion protection is a long-term joint conversation no one wants to have mid-season. Worth it for short sessions. Not for extended vertical training.

### Wide Footer — NOT IT
The narrow last is not a fit suggestion — it is the shape of the shoe. Wide footers cannot make this work. A foot that doesn't seat correctly in this shoe defeats every performance advantage it offers. Traction becomes unreliable. Containment becomes counterproductive. The Kobe 4 Protro demands the right foot. If yours isn't it, this is not your shoe.

### Outdoor Hooper — SHAKY
The outsole on the Protro is built for indoor hardwood — the herringbone excels there. Outdoor courts will wear the pattern at a rate that accelerates past indoor timelines significantly. The thin cushion on hard concrete is also more punishing than the shoe's design intent. Occasional outdoor use is survivable. Dedicated outdoor player? This is not your first choice.

## FINAL VERDICT

**STURDY — for players who earn it.**

The Kobe 4 Protro is not for everyone and it never pretended to be. The traction and court feel are the best combination available in a basketball shoe right now — full stop. But this shoe requires the right foot, the right size, the right ankle trust, and the right weight class to deliver on its promise. Get any of those wrong and you're not wearing a legend — you're wearing a liability. Get them right? You're playing basketball in its purest form. The floor tells you everything. You just have to be built to listen.
