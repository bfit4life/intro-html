## ARCHETYPE MATCH

### Shifty Guard (140-185 lbs) — STURDY
The OG-1 is slightly heavier than a pure guard shoe, but the FuelCell energy return compensates for the weight penalty. Traction handles quick decel and lateral cuts. True-to-size medium width means your foot is seated correctly. The containment is real. Guards will give up a small amount of raw court feel versus thinner Zoom setups but gain cushion durability in return. For guards who want more cushion without sacrificing agility, this is a strong option.

### Explosive Wing (185-225 lbs) — STURDY
This is the primary target. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is the blueprint — scoring wing, creation off the dribble, pull-up threat from everywhere. The OG-1 covers the full wing movement profile. FuelCell handles the impact loads. Containment is real under wing-level lateral forces. Traction holds through wing play patterns. This archetype gets the full benefit of every design decision in this shoe. Primary recommendation.

### Heavy Forward (215-270+ lbs) — SHAKY
FuelCell is more weight-tolerant than React or Phylon, which earns this a SHAKY rather than a hard NOT IT. Forwards in the 215-235 range playing a perimeter-heavy game might function in these. Interior-heavy forwards with post footwork, body contact, and repeated jump landings will eventually find the FuelCell ceiling. The torsion structure is not built for the lateral forces a true heavy forward generates. Proceed cautiously and monitor cushion performance actively.

### Older Hooper — STURDY
This is the OG-1's most underrated performance category. FuelCell's energy return is a genuine joint-protection feature — it reduces cumulative impact loading compared to flat foam alternatives. The medium-width accommodating fit means older hoopers with feet that have changed shape over time can get seated correctly. The traction is reliable enough to trust on every cut. Older players who need cushion support without sacrificing court competitiveness — the OG-1 is a serious recommendation.

### Volleyball-Style Mover — STURDY
FuelCell was designed for energy return in a vertical-load context. Basketball is the application — volleyball-style movement is the purest test of that design. Forefoot loading on jump approach, vertical impact on landing, repeated cycles of this pattern — FuelCell handles this better than React or Phylon. Traction holds on forefoot landing patterns. The OG-1 is a surprise strong recommendation for this archetype.

### Wide Footer — STURDY
New Balance's fit engineering legacy shows up here in a significant way. The medium-width OG-1 accommodates a genuine range of foot shapes. Wide footers who have been locked out of guard and wing shoes due to narrow lasts now have a competitive performance option. If your foot is wide-to-medium, you may find the OG-1 is the first serious basketball shoe you've been able to actually play in without fighting the fit. This matters more than any other category for this archetype.

### Outdoor Hooper — SHAKY
The outsole is built for indoor hardwood — clean court performance is the baseline. Outdoor courts will wear the traction pattern faster than the indoor use case suggests. FuelCell on concrete is more punishing than the cushion data implies — harder surfaces extract more energy from the foam over time. Occasional outdoor use is workable. Full-time outdoor player — the OG-1's longevity outdoors doesn't match its indoor capability. Not the pick for your outdoor runs.

## FINAL VERDICT

**STURDY — and the category should be paying attention.**

New Balance came back to basketball with their best foot forward and the OG-1 earns legitimate respect on the performance matrix. For most archetypes, that's an acceptable trade. For wings, older hoopers, and wide footers specifically, the OG-1 is not just an option — it is the recommendation. New Balance is back.
