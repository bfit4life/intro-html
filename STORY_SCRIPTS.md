# PILLAR 4: COURT INTELLIGENCE
### Teaching moments. 15-30 seconds. Simple camera. High trust.

---

**SCRIPT 4.1 — Why Herringbone Works**

**CATEGORY:** Court Intelligence

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Outsole of the KD 4 flat on the court floor, Bryan's hand pointing to specific sections of the herringbone pattern as he explains. Simple, still camera.

**AUDIO:** Bryan talking. No music. Room-tone ambient underneath.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:**
- [0:00] "COURT INTELLIGENCE"
- [0:02] "Why herringbone is the standard."

**SPOKEN/CAPTION TEXT:**
"Herringbone is everywhere for a reason. The angled channels cut into the rubber in alternating directions — that means you get grip no matter which way you're moving. Forward, lateral, diagonal, stopping — the pattern has an edge for every angle. Multidirectional patterns are more specialized. Circular patterns are for specific court conditions. But herringbone? It works everywhere. That's why it's been on basketball outsoles since the 80s and it's still here."

**PLATFORM NOTE:** Both

---

**SCRIPT 4.2 — Heavy Hoopers & Foam Compression**

**CATEGORY:** Court Intelligence

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Side-by-side: Bryan pressing his thumb hard into a midsole (compressed) vs. pressing lightly (barely deforms). Camera close on the foam. Simple, illustrative.

**AUDIO:** Clean voiceover. No music.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:**
- [0:00] "COURT INTELLIGENCE"
- [0:02] "Why heavy hoopers bottom out foam faster."

**SPOKEN/CAPTION TEXT:**
"Foam has a compression threshold. Every midsole is rated to absorb a certain amount of force before it maxes out — before you're feeling court through the shoe. If a shoe is designed for a 170-pound guard and you're 230, you are bottoming out that foam. Every landing. The foam isn't broken — it's just not built for your load. This is why two players can wear the same shoe and have completely different experiences. Size, weight, play style, position — it all affects how cushion performs on your foot specifically."

**PLATFORM NOTE:** Both

---

**SCRIPT 4.3 — Wide Footers & Narrow Bases**

**CATEGORY:** Court Intelligence

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Bryan holding up two different shoes side by side — one visibly wider base (like the Shai OG-1) vs. a narrow one (like the Ja 3). Camera switches to close-up of the toe boxes.

**AUDIO:** Clean voiceover, slight ambient.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:**
- [0:00] "COURT INTELLIGENCE"
- [0:03] "Wide footers. Narrow bases. The problem."

**SPOKEN/CAPTION TEXT:**
"If you have a wide foot and you're forcing it into a narrow toe box, you're losing stability. Here's why: your foot spreads when you land. It's supposed to. That spread creates your base. If the shoe is fighting that spread — restricting it — you lose the natural stability your foot would give you, and the shoe can't compensate. Result: less secure plant, less confident cut, more ankle risk over time. Know your foot shape. Match the shoe. This matters more than any traction rating."

**PLATFORM NOTE:** Both

---

**SCRIPT 4.4 — Court Feel & Movement Behavior**

**CATEGORY:** Court Intelligence

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Bryan dribbling on the baseline, then stopping. He bounces on his toes in the shoe, testing feel. Camera low, near floor level. You see the court, the shoe, the slight flex.

**AUDIO:** Court ambient. Dribble. His voice over the top.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:**
- [0:00] "COURT INTELLIGENCE"
- [0:03] "Court feel changes how you move."

**SPOKEN/CAPTION TEXT:**
"Court feel isn't just comfort — it's information. When you can feel the court through your shoe, your brain is getting real-time data about your surface, your footing, your traction. High-stack shoes cut that signal. You feel less. That sounds like comfort but it can actually slow your reaction time on cuts and stops because your body has less information to work with. Low-profile shoes give you more signal. The trade is cushion. Neither is wrong — it's a choice. Know what you're trading."

**PLATFORM NOTE:** Both

---

**SCRIPT 4.5 — Outsole Rubber Durometer**

**CATEGORY:** Court Intelligence

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Bryan showing two outsoles — one visibly harder, one softer — pressing into each. The difference in give is visible on camera. Close and simple.

**AUDIO:** Bryan explaining directly. No music. Clean mic.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:**
- [0:00] "COURT INTELLIGENCE"
- [0:03] "Nobody talks about rubber hardness."
- [0:06] "They should."

**SPOKEN/CAPTION TEXT:**
"Durometer is the hardness rating of outsole rubber. Softer rubber grips harder. Also wears faster. Harder rubber lasts longer. Grips less. Most brands don't publish this number. You figure it out by testing. Soft rubber on a clean court feels incredible — immediate bite. Soft rubber on a dusty court doesn't help you because it's picking up dust particles and losing contact. Hard rubber is more consistent across surface conditions. This is why indoor shoes and outdoor shoes are different products. The rubber is different. On purpose."

**PLATFORM NOTE:** Both
