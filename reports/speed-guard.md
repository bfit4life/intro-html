### Your Profile, Expanded

Your game is built on pace. You push every rebound into transition, you beat defenders before they're set, and you live in the first four seconds of a possession. Your shoe's job isn't to make you fast — it's to not steal speed you already have. Every extra ounce on your foot is a tax on your best asset.

**Your NBA Comp:** De'Aaron Fox, John Wall, Ja Morant — players whose entire offensive identity is "get there before the defense exists." Notice how low-profile their signature shoes tend to be relative to bigger teammates' shoes.

### The Mechanical Breakdown — Why Your Shoe Requirements Are Different

Every ounce of shoe weight costs you measurable stride efficiency over a 40-minute game — the effect compounds with fatigue. Most players chase "cushioning" as the top priority, but for a Speed Guard, excess cushioning is actually a liability: soft foam compresses on push-off and returns energy slower than your foot wants to move. You need responsive, not soft. Responsive cushioning returns energy fast enough to match your stride cadence; soft cushioning just absorbs and sits there.

Your priority order should be: **weight first, responsiveness second, traction third, lockdown fourth.**

## The Traction Report Fit-Testing Protocol

1. **Bring your real socks.** Thin dress socks change the fit entirely.
2. **Walk, don't just stand.** Walk the store. Jog in place.
3. **Lateral shuffle test.** 5 defensive slides each direction. Foot shift or heel lift = fit failure.
4. **Thumb-width toe test.** A thumb's width between your longest toe and the shoe's front.
5. **Heel-lock test.** Lace fully, try to lift your heel out. More than a few millimeters = inadequate lockdown.
6. **Simulate your actual movement.** Sprint a few steps and stop hard right in the store.
7. **Give it 10 minutes** before deciding.
8. **Go true to size first**, then adjust — basketball sizing often differs from lifestyle sneakers.
