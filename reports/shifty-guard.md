### The Mechanical Breakdown — Why Your Shoe Requirements Are Different

When you plant for a crossover, your foot experiences lateral shear force — your foot wants to slide sideways relative to the outsole. Two things stop that: (1) the traction pattern's grip on the floor, and (2) how tightly the upper holds your foot to the footbed so your foot doesn't slide *inside* the shoe even if the outsole grips. Most "good" basketball shoes optimize for #1 and ignore #2. That's why you can wear a shoe with great reviews and still feel like your foot is "swimming" on a hard cut — the outsole isn't the problem, the lockdown is.

This is why your priority order should be: **heel lockdown first, traction pattern second, weight third, cushion last.**

## The Traction Report Fit-Testing Protocol

*Use this every time you try on a shoe — new releases come out constantly and this works on anything.*

1. **Bring your real socks.** Thin dress socks change the fit entirely.
2. **Walk, don't just stand.** Walk the store. Jog in place. Fit reveals itself in motion.
3. **Lateral shuffle test.** Do 5 defensive slides each direction in the store. Foot shift or heel lift = fit failure, not a "break it in" situation.
4. **Thumb-width toe test.** A thumb's width between your longest toe and the shoe's front when laced normally.
5. **Heel-lock test.** Lace fully, try to lift your heel out. More than a few millimeters of rise = the shoe lacks lockdown for your foot shape.
6. **Simulate your actual movement.** Do a hard crossover step right in the store. Most fit problems only show up under your specific game motion.
7. **Give it 10 minutes** before deciding. Discomfort that fades is newness. Discomfort that stays is a real problem.
8. **Go true to size first**, then adjust using this protocol — basketball sizing often differs from lifestyle sneaker sizing in the same brand.
