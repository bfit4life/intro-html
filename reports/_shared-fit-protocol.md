# The Traction Report Fit-Testing Protocol

*This page is included in every Shoe Match Report. Use it every time you try on a shoe, regardless of what's on this list — new releases come out constantly and this protocol works on any shoe.*

**1. Bring your real socks.** The socks you actually hoop in. Thin dress socks change the fit entirely.

**2. Walk, don't just stand.** Most people stand in a shoe for 10 seconds and decide. Walk the length of the store. Then jog in place. Fit reveals itself in motion, not in stillness.

**3. Do the lateral shuffle test.** Right in the store, do 5 defensive slides each direction. If your foot shifts inside the shoe or your heel lifts, that's a fit failure — not a "break it in" situation.

**4. The thumb-width toe test.** With the shoe laced normally, you should be able to fit roughly a thumb's width between your longest toe and the front of the shoe. Less than that and the shoe is too small for explosive movement (toe jam on hard stops). More than that and you'll have instability on cuts.

**5. The heel-lock test.** Lace the shoe fully, then try to lift your heel out while keeping the laces tight. If your heel rises more than a few millimeters, the shoe doesn't have proper lockdown for your foot shape — no amount of break-in fixes this.

**6. Simulate your actual movement.** If you're a guard, do a hard crossover step in the store. If you're a big, do a drop-step. If you're a defender, do a closeout sprint-and-stop. Most fit problems only show up under the specific motion you actually use in games.

**7. Give it 10 minutes before deciding.** Discomfort that's still there after 10 minutes of movement is a real fit problem. Discomfort that fades is just newness.

**8. When in doubt, go true to size first — then adjust based on this protocol, not based on the size you wear in casual sneakers.** Basketball shoe sizing often runs differently than lifestyle sneakers from the same brand.
