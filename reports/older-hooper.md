### Your Profile, Expanded

Age is a basketball asset nobody talks about. Your feel for the game, your anticipation, your footwork, your IQ — these compound over decades. What doesn't compound is joint recovery time. The rookie next to you can play five days straight and feel nothing; you can't, and that's fine, because every game is an investment now. The wrong shoe means knees that ache Monday and a back that protests Wednesday. The right shoe means you're still playing at 60% physical capacity every time out — and 60% of your game beats most people's 100%.

**Your NBA Comp:** LeBron James (at 39+), Chris Paul, Vince Carter in his final seasons — players whose longevity was a footwear decision as much as a genetic one.

### The Mechanical Breakdown — Why Your Shoe Requirements Are Different

Cartilage and tendon recovery slow with age regardless of fitness level, which means the cushioning that "felt fine" at 25 needs to do more work at 40+. You need superior heel and impact cushioning, built-in arch support, and a roomy toe box — narrow fits that didn't bother you a decade ago will cause real pain now. Lightweight matters too: every extra ounce is fatigue you're carrying for four quarters instead of two.

Your priority order should be: **cushioning and joint protection first, arch support second, fit comfort third, weight fourth.**

## The Traction Report Fit-Testing Protocol

1. **Bring your real socks** — and any orthotic inserts you currently use.
2. **Walk, don't just stand.**
3. **Lateral shuffle test** — 5 slides each direction, paying attention to any ankle hesitation.
4. **Thumb-width toe test.**
5. **Heel-lock test.**
6. **Simulate your actual movement** — a post-up drop step or a catch-and-shoot, whatever your game actually uses.
7. **Give it 15 minutes**, not just 10 — older joints need more time to register discomfort.
8. **Go true to size first, then size up if needed** — a roomier toe box is rarely a mistake for this archetype.
