### The Mechanical Breakdown — Why Your Shoe Requirements Are Different

A step-back generates more torque through the midfoot than almost any other basketball movement — you're decelerating, rotating, and pushing off in a different direction within a fraction of a second. This requires a shoe with enough cushion to absorb that deceleration without going dead (too-thin soles transmit shock straight into your knees over the volume of reps you take) but enough structure that you don't feel unstable mid-rotation.

Your priority order should be: **traction consistency first, moderate cushioning second, flexible forefoot third, comfort for high-volume wear fourth.**

## The Traction Report Fit-Testing Protocol

1. **Bring your real socks.**
2. **Walk, don't just stand.**
3. **Lateral shuffle test** — 5 slides each direction.
4. **Thumb-width toe test.**
5. **Heel-lock test** — lift heel test with laces tight.
6. **Simulate your actual movement** — do a jab step and step-back right in the store.
7. **Give it 10 minutes** before deciding.
8. **Go true to size first**, then adjust.
