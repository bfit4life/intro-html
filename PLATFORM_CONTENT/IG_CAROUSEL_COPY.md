**SLIDE 2 — THE SHIFTY GUARD**

Headline: THE SHIFTY GUARD

Body: Quick. Change-of-direction player. Lives in the paint, lives on the perimeter. Your whole game is reading the defense and reacting before it adjusts. You need: Maximum traction grip. Low-to-medium stack for court feel. Flexible forefoot for quick cuts.

What to look for: Full herringbone traction. Flexible midsole. No excessive heel bias.

Visual direction: Silhouette of a player mid-crossover. Single accent color (TTR brand color).

---

**SLIDE 3 — THE EXPLOSIVE WING**

Headline: THE EXPLOSIVE WING

Body: First step is a weapon. You get vertical. You attack the rim. The game starts with your explosiveness and ends with how well your shoe returns your energy. You need: Responsive cushion that gives energy back. Strong containment on takeoff. Heel lockdown for full-power launch.

What to look for: Energy-return foam (not just thick foam). Lace-to-toe lockdown. Rigid heel counter.

Visual direction: Silhouette mid-air, reaching for rim. Upward visual momentum.

---

**SLIDE 4 — THE HEAVY FORWARD**

Headline: THE HEAVY FORWARD

Body: You're physical. You post up. You set screens that leave marks. Your weight distribution is different — and your shoe needs to carry that load across a full game. You need: Wide base for stability. Maximum cushion stack for joint protection. Durable outsole that doesn't break down under repeated heavy impact.

What to look for: Wide toebox or wide-available sizing. High-stack cushion. Reinforced outsole.

Visual direction: Low angle shot of a wider base shoe on court. Power aesthetic.

---

**SLIDE 5 — THE OLDER HOOPER**

Headline: THE OLDER HOOPER

Body: You're smarter than everyone else on the court. You've also got more years logged on your joints, and your body doesn't bounce back the way it used to. You need: Joint protection above everything. Cushion that holds under fatigue. Ankle support that keeps you on the court longer. You need a shoe that extends your game, not ends it.

What to look for: Maximum cushion stack. Mid-to-high cut. Foam that doesn't break down after minute 45.

Visual direction: Veteran player, calm and deliberate. Wisdom visual — not a decline narrative.

---

**SLIDE 6 — THE WIDE FOOTER / OUTDOOR HOOPER**

Headline: THE WIDE FOOTER + THE OUTDOOR HOOPER

Body (Wide Footer): If your feet spill over the midsole edge — you are a Wide Footer. Fit comes before everything. A narrow shoe in the wrong width can create lateral roll risk no matter how good the rest of the shoe is. Look for: Wide sizing availability. Deep toebox. Stable base.

Body (Outdoor Hooper): You're on concrete. Asphalt. Surfaces that chew through rubber in weeks. Durability is your traction. Look for: Hard rubber compound. Thicker outsole depth. Durable upper materials.

Visual direction: Split slide — wide foot on court (one side), outdoor court texture (other side).

**SLIDE 7 — THE REVEAL**

Headline: KNOW YOUR ARCHETYPE. KNOW YOUR SHOE.

## CAROUSEL 2: THE PERFORMANCE MATRIX
### What TTR Tests and Why — 7 Slides

---

**SLIDE 1 — COVER**

Headline: HOW WE TEST BASKETBALL SHOES

Body: 7 categories. Real court conditions. No opinions without data.

Subhead: The TTR Performance Matrix

Visual direction: Lab/technical aesthetic. Grid lines. Scientific feel. Bold white text on black.

---

**SLIDE 2 — MOVEMENT EFFICIENCY**

Headline: 1. MOVEMENT EFFICIENCY

Body: Can the shoe keep up with your game? We test: How quickly it responds to direction changes. Whether the sole flex matches natural foot movement. If the forefoot is stiff where it shouldn't be.

Why it matters: A shoe that fights your natural movement pattern costs you steps. Fractions of seconds. Games.

Visual direction: Diagram of foot flex points. Clean graphic, lab aesthetic.

---

**SLIDE 3 — TRACTION AND GRIP**

Headline: 2. TRACTION + GRIP

Body: This is not just "did it slip." We test: Grip on clean hardwood. Grip on dusty hardwood. Grip on outdoor surfaces. Grip after 60 minutes of wear. Because traction that cracks under fatigue is not traction at all.

Why it matters: One slip at the wrong moment is a blown play. One slip on the wrong surface is an injury.

Visual direction: Close-up of shoe sole on court. Tread pattern highlighted. Clean.

---

**SLIDE 4 — CONTAINMENT UNDER PRESSURE**

Headline: 3. CONTAINMENT UNDER PRESSURE

Body: Does the shoe hold your foot when you cut hard? We test: Lateral containment under full-speed cuts. Heel slippage during acceleration. Foot migration inside the shoe — invisible until it costs you.

Why it matters: Containment failure feels like ankle instability. Most people blame their ankle. The shoe is the variable.

Visual direction: Diagram of lateral cut force direction. Arrow graphics showing foot position inside shoe.

---

**SLIDE 5 — PERFORMANCE SUSTAINABILITY**

Headline: 4. PERFORMANCE SUSTAINABILITY

Body: Does the shoe perform the same at minute 60 as it did at minute 10? We test traction, cushion response, and containment across a full playing session. We document when things change.

Why it matters: Shoes are tested fresh. Games are not played fresh. The real test is what happens when everyone's tired.

Visual direction: Time graph showing performance curve — "first 20 min" vs "minute 60 vs minute 90." Simple line graphic.

---

**SLIDE 6 — RECOVERY AND JOINT PROTECTION**

Headline: 5. RECOVERY + JOINT PROTECTION

Body: What does your body feel after wearing this shoe? We test: Foam compression retention. Ankle fatigue accumulation. Post-session joint feedback. The thumb test (press the insole — how fast does it bounce back?).

Why it matters: The best performance shoe is the one that keeps you on the court next week. Not just this week.

Visual direction: Bryan pressing thumb into insole. Text overlay with thumb test instructions.

**SLIDE 7 — THE VERDICT**

Headline: EVERY TEST ENDS WITH A VERDICT

Body: STURDY — Built for the court. Handles the game. SHAKY — Situational. Depends on your archetype and court conditions. NOT IT — Don't risk your game on this shoe.

No 7-out-of-10s. No hedging. No affiliate pressure. Just a verdict you can trust.

## CAROUSEL 3: STURDY | SHAKY | NOT IT — HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS
### 8 Slides

---

**SLIDE 1 — COVER**

Headline: STURDY. SHAKY. NOT IT.

Body: Three verdicts. No more 7-out-of-10s. Here's how the system works — and why it matters.

Visual direction: All three words on screen at once, stacked. Bold. Clean. Unforgettable.

---

**SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM WITH 7/10**

Headline: EVERY SHOE IS A 7/10

Body: Watch enough sneaker reviews and you'll notice: almost every shoe gets a 7 or 8 out of 10. Even the bad ones. Even the ones that don't grip. Even the ones that break down in 6 weeks. Why? Because the reviewer got them for free. Because the brand might send more.

The number scale is broken when financial incentives are involved.

Visual direction: "7/10" graphic crossed out in red. Clean, provocative.

---

**SLIDE 3 — HOW WE BUILT THE VERDICT SYSTEM**

Headline: WE NEEDED SOMETHING BINARY. ALMOST.

Body: A shoe either handles your game — or it doesn't. STURDY means it handles it. NOT IT means it doesn't. But some shoes are situational. Good for one archetype, wrong for another. Good indoors, dangerous outdoors. For those shoes — SHAKY. Not a cop-out. A specific answer for a specific question.

Visual direction: Three category boxes with brief descriptions. Grid layout, clean.

---

**SLIDE 4 — WHAT "STURDY" MEANS**

Headline: STURDY

Body: A STURDY verdict means the shoe delivered across our testing matrix for the archetype it was tested with. Traction held. Containment was real. Cushion survived fatigue. We trust it for the game. STURDY doesn't mean perfect. It means the shoe earns the court.

Examples of STURDY: Nike Zoom Freak 5 for Explosive Wings. Air Jordan 37 for Heavy Forwards. Adidas Dame 8 for Shifty Guards.

Visual direction: STURDY in large white bold text. Underlining or badge graphic. Green accent.

---

**SLIDE 5 — WHAT "SHAKY" MEANS**

Headline: SHAKY

Body: SHAKY means: proceed with information. A SHAKY verdict usually comes with a condition. "SHAKY for Outdoor Hoopers — the compound wears too fast." "SHAKY for Wide Footers — containment fails on lateral cuts." "SHAKY for Older Hoopers — cushion doesn't recover under sustained load." SHAKY is not a bad verdict. It's a specific verdict. Know your conditions. Make an informed decision.

Visual direction: SHAKY in yellow/amber. Split "good" and "bad" condition visual. Warning signal aesthetic without alarm.

---

**SLIDE 6 — WHAT "NOT IT" MEANS**

Headline: NOT IT

Body: NOT IT is a verdict we don't give lightly. It means the shoe failed in ways that aren't situational. Traction that creates slip risk. Containment that actually increases ankle instability. Foam that compresses to nothing inside 30 minutes. NOT IT means: keep your money. Try something else. We'll tell you what.

Visual direction: NOT IT in red. No hedging in the copy. Direct.

---

**SLIDE 7 — WHY THE SYSTEM IS ARCHETYPE-LINKED**

Headline: ONE SHOE. MULTIPLE VERDICTS.

Body: A shoe can be STURDY for one archetype and NOT IT for another. That's not a contradiction. That's the point. The Kobe 8 is STURDY for Shifty Guards. It's SHAKY for Heavy Forwards. It's NOT IT for Older Hoopers who need joint protection. The right verdict depends on who's wearing the shoe. That's why we never give you just one number.

Visual direction: Three archetype labels with different verdicts for same shoe. Visual matrix showing the split.

---

**SLIDE 8 — THE COMMITMENT**

Headline: NO FREE SHOES CHANGE THE VERDICT

Body: TTR doesn't accept free shoes from brands. Not yet. When we do, you'll know — because we'll disclose it, and the verdict will still be based on the data from the test. A brand sending us a shoe doesn't buy a STURDY. The court decides. The data decides. We report it.

CTA: Follow The Traction Report. Verdicts you can trust. Always.
