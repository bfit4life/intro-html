## CATEGORY 3: PERFORMANCE OBSERVATIONS (3 Posts)
*Real testing intel. Establishes credibility.*

---

**PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION #1**

Observation from this week's testing:

The herringbone traction patterns that bite hardest on clean hardwood are also the ones that clog fastest on dusty courts.

There is no perfect traction solution. There are only smart trade-offs.

Know what surface you play on. Buy for that surface.

---

**PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION #2**

I tested three shoes with "cushion" in their marketing this week.

One of them had less foam response after 45 minutes than a 3-year-old pair of training shoes I've had in my closet.

"Cushion" is a marketing word. Foam retention over time is a performance metric. Different things.

---

**PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION #3**

Something nobody talks about: Wide footers are not just buying a bigger size.

A wide foot in a narrow shoe creates lateral roll risk. The foot hangs over the midsole edge. That's not a sizing issue — it's an engineering mismatch.

If your shoes feel tight across the top of your foot, you are a Wide Footer. Your shoe options are different. We cover this.

**EDUCATIONAL QUICK HIT #1**

Three things that actually determine basketball shoe traction:

1. Pattern geometry (herringbone vs. multidirectional vs. circular)
2. Rubber compound hardness (soft bites harder, wears faster)
3. Court surface and dust conditions

All three matter. Reviews that only describe the pattern are giving you one-third of the information.

---

**EDUCATIONAL QUICK HIT #2**

The thumb test.

After a long run, take your shoe off. Press your thumb firmly into the insole foam.

If it bounces back in 2-3 seconds — the foam still has life.

If your thumbprint stays for 5+ seconds — the cushion is compressed. Your joints absorbed what the foam was supposed to.

Test your shoes. Know what you're actually hooping in.
