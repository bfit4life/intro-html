**THE PROBLEM WITH EVERY SNEAKER REVIEW YOU'VE EVER WATCHED**

*By Bryan Wilson, The Traction Report*

---

Let me describe a video you've seen a hundred times.

A guy opens a box. He pulls out a shoe wrapped in tissue paper like it's a newborn. He holds it up to the camera and says, "Oh, these are *clean.*" He spends three minutes describing the upper material, the colorway, the stitching detail on the heel. He puts the shoe on, takes a few steps, and says it "feels really comfortable." He gives it an 8 out of 10. He puts an affiliate link in the description.

That's a sneaker review. And there's nothing wrong with it — for the audience it's built for. Collectors love that content. Hypebeasts live for it. People who buy shoes to display them on shelves or post them on Instagram get real value from knowing exactly what the colorway looks like in natural light.

But you play basketball.

And that video just told you almost nothing about whether that shoe will hold up when the game gets hard.

---

Here's what a sneaker review does not tell you:

It doesn't tell you how the traction performs on a dusty gym floor at 8pm on a Tuesday — which is the actual condition of most pickup games. It doesn't tell you what the foam does at minute 60 when your feet are fatigued and the cushion has been compressing under your weight for an hour. It doesn't tell you whether the ankle collar will actually contain your foot on a hard lateral cut or whether your foot will migrate inside the shoe and you'll feel it in your ankle for three days.

It doesn't tell you any of that because it was never tested for any of that.

The standard sneaker review tests the shoe the way a car reviewer tests a sports car by looking at it in a showroom. The aesthetics are real. The performance under actual conditions is speculation.

---

The problem gets worse.

Most shoe reviewers receive the products for free. I understand this — building a media brand costs money and free shoes are a legitimate form of compensation. But it creates a structural pressure. If you give the shoe a NOT IT verdict, the brand stops sending shoes. So the scores cluster — always 7s and 8s, rarely below a 6, never a real "don't buy this." The incentive to be honest directly conflicts with the incentive to keep the content machine running.

I don't have a solution for how other people run their channels. I only know what I'm not willing to do with mine.

---

The Traction Report exists because there's a specific question that wasn't being answered.

Not "is this shoe aesthetically appealing?" Not "does this shoe live up to the hype?" But: *Will this shoe hold up in the 4th quarter of a real game, for a real hooper, on a real court?*

The answer depends on who the hooper is. A 6'8" power forward and a 5'9" point guard do not need the same shoe. They put different force on the sole. They need support in different places. They play for different lengths of time at different intensities. The same shoe can be the right call for one and genuinely dangerous for the other.

No one was mapping that. No one was doing the archetype-specific fatigue testing. No one was giving a verdict that actually helped the competitive recreational player make a real decision.

That gap is what The Traction Report is for.

---

We don't review sneakers. We lab them.

That means: court testing, not parking lot testing. Real play conditions, not a studio with good lighting. Fatigue data, not just first impressions. An honest verdict at the end — STURDY, SHAKY, or NOT IT — with no affiliate pressure attached.

If you play the game seriously, you deserve content that takes your game seriously.

That's what we're building.

*— Bryan Wilson*
*Founder, The Traction Report*

## ARTICLE 2: "Which Basketball Shoe Actually Fits Your Game?"

**WHICH BASKETBALL SHOE ACTUALLY FITS YOUR GAME?**

*An Introduction to The TTR Archetype System*

*By Bryan Wilson, The Traction Report*

---

The question gets asked every week. Every time a new shoe drops, every time a performance shoe goes on sale, every time a hooper is standing in the store with two boxes on the floor and $150 to spend:

*Which one should I get?*

The honest answer — the one most reviewers won't give you because it's complicated — is: *it depends on what kind of player you are.*

Not depends on your budget. Not depends on the colorway you prefer. Depends on your movement profile. Your body type. Your court surface. How many minutes you play at a time and how many games you play per week. Whether your ankles have a history or your knees need extra cushion or your wide feet have been fighting narrow toe boxes for years.

The TTR Archetype System is our answer to this problem. Seven movement profiles. Seven different performance priorities. And a clear framework for matching a shoe to the player, not just the trend.

---

**THE SEVEN ARCHETYPES**

**The Shifty Guard** — You live by change of direction. Your crossover is your first language. You need a shoe that responds as fast as you do: maximum traction grip, a flexible forefoot, low-to-medium cushion stack for court feel, and containment that locks your foot without slowing your reads. What you don't need: excessive cushion that dulls your connection to the floor.

**The Explosive Wing** — Your first step is a weapon. You get vertical. You attack the rim in straight lines. You need responsive cushion that gives energy back on takeoff, not foam that absorbs your explosiveness. Heel lockdown and forefoot responsiveness are critical. The shoe needs to launch with you.

**The Heavy Forward** — You're physical. Post-up game. Screens, boards, contact. Your weight creates more downward force with every step than any other archetype, which means your cushion stack degrades faster, your outsole takes more punishment, and your joints need more protection. Wide base, maximum stack, durable outsole. These are not luxury features for you. They're structural requirements.

**The Older Hooper** — You're smarter than everyone else on the court and you've got the joint history to prove it. Recovery and protection are your priorities. The shoe needs to keep you on the court for the full run *and* let you show up next week. Maximum cushion that retains response under load. Ankle support. Foam that doesn't break down at minute 45. Your knees are doing the math even when you aren't.

**The Volleyball-Style Mover** — You play defense the way a volleyball player moves: multi-directional, reactive, quick drops into athletic stances. Standard basketball shoes are built for linear and lateral movement. Your movement adds a vertical-reactive component that changes what you need from a lateral stability plate and forefoot design.

**The Wide Footer** — Fit is your first performance question. If your foot is hanging over the midsole edge, no amount of great cushion or traction matters. Lateral roll risk starts at the fit. Wide sizing availability is non-negotiable. Deep toebox. Stable base. Everything else is secondary until the fit is right.

**The Outdoor Hooper** — Concrete and asphalt chew through basketball shoe rubber in weeks. Your first question is durability. Hard rubber compound. Thicker outsole depth. Reinforced upper materials. A "performance" shoe built for hardwood isn't built for your court. You need a different set of priorities.

---

**WHY THIS MATTERS FOR BUYING DECISIONS**

A shoe can be STURDY for one archetype and NOT IT for another.

The Kobe 8 — one of the most beloved basketball shoes ever made — is a legitimate performance shoe for Shifty Guards and Explosive Wings. Low cut, court-feel forward, responsive. That's a real shoe for a real type of player. Put that same shoe on a Heavy Forward or an Older Hooper who needs joint protection, and the limited stack becomes a liability over a full game.

Neither verdict is wrong. The shoe didn't change. The player did.

This is what TTR's testing methodology is built around. We don't test shoes as generic objects. We test them against specific movement profiles in real fatigue conditions. The result isn't one verdict — it's a verdict with context. STURDY for the Shifty Guard. SHAKY for the Older Hooper. NOT IT for the Heavy Forward on back-to-back game nights.

That specificity is what makes the data useful. That's the difference between a review and a lab.

---

**FINDING YOUR ARCHETYPE**

In the next article, we're building the full archetype self-assessment — a series of questions about how you play that will land you in the right profile.

But for now, start simple: What do you rely on most?

Speed and reads? You're probably a Shifty Guard. First step and athleticism? Explosive Wing. Size and physicality? Heavy Forward. Smarts and experience over athleticism? Older Hooper. Volume and durability? Outdoor Hooper. Fit above all else? Wide Footer.

Most players have a primary archetype with secondary characteristics. A Shifty Guard who's getting older starts needing some Older Hooper cushion priorities. A Heavy Forward who plays outdoor needs durability stacked on top of joint protection.

The point isn't rigid categorization. The point is giving you a framework for evaluating shoes against *your* game — instead of a number between 1 and 10 that doesn't tell you anything specific.
