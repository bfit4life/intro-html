## SCRIPT 1: "WHY I STARTED THE TRACTION REPORT"
### Channel Intro Video | 3-5 Minute Equivalent | Bryan's Personal Story

---

**[VISUAL: Open on Bryan on a basketball court. No fancy setup. Just a court, natural light, Bryan in basketball shoes. He's holding a basketball. He speaks directly to camera. No intro music. No graphics. Just him.]**

---

**SECTION 1: THE HOOK (0:00 - 0:30)**

I need to tell you why this channel exists. Because it's not what you think.

I didn't start The Traction Report because I love sneakers.

I started it because I got tired of spending real money on basketball shoes based on advice that had nothing to do with actually playing basketball.

**[Bryan bounces the ball once. Stops it. Looks at camera.]**

That's it. That's the whole origin story. But let me give you the longer version.

---

**SECTION 2: THE PROBLEM (0:30 - 1:30)**

**[Bryan walks slowly toward camera — intimate, conversational energy]**

I play. I've been playing for a long time. Pickup games. Rec leagues. Sunday runs. That thing where you show up and you don't know who's going to be there but you know it's going to be competitive — that's my world.

And I take it seriously. I take care of my body, I watch film, I work on my game. And part of taking the game seriously is taking your gear seriously.

So I'd go looking for information. Which shoes should I buy? Which ones are actually built for someone who plays the way I play?

**[Bryan stops walking. Leans in.]**

And every review I found was... fine. The shoe looks great. The colorway is clean. Here's the unboxing. Here's a couple laps around the gym. Here's an 8 out of 10.

That didn't help me at all.

It didn't tell me what the traction actually does on a dusty floor at 8pm when the court hasn't been mopped in three days. It didn't tell me what the cushion feels like at minute 60 when I've been playing hard and my feet are swollen. It didn't tell me whether this shoe is actually built for my body, my movement, the way I attack the game.

And I'm not a 22-year-old with fresh knees and no mileage. I need real data. Not vibes.

**[Bryan sits on the court. Camera stays on his face.]**

The other thing I noticed — and this took me a while to fully understand — is that the reviewers who were getting closest to real performance data were still treating every basketball player as the same person.

But I'm not the same player as the guy who's 6'7" and 240 pounds who plays in the post. I don't need the same shoe he needs. I cut differently. I load the sole differently. I need lateral containment in a way that's completely different from what he needs for joint protection on downward force.

Nobody was talking about that. Nobody was mapping the shoe to the player.

---

**SECTION 3: THE IDEA (1:30 - 2:30)**

**[Bryan stands back up. More energy now — this is where the idea crystallized.]**

So I started keeping notes.

Nothing fancy. Just: what happened in this shoe? How did my feet feel at the end of the run? Did I slip? Did my ankle feel unstable? Did the cushion feel dead by the end of the game?

Over time those notes became a system. I started building criteria. Traction. Containment. Fatigue performance. Recovery. Archetype matching — that was the big one.

Seven player archetypes. Seven different movement profiles. Different bodies, different positions, different demands on the shoe. And for each shoe, the question wasn't just "is this a good shoe" — it was "is this a good shoe for *this type of player*?"

**[Beat. Bryan holds up one finger.]**

The Shifty Guard doesn't need the same shoe as the Heavy Forward.

**[Two fingers.]**

The Older Hooper doesn't need the same shoe as the Explosive Wing.

**[Three fingers.]**

The Wide Footer can't even evaluate a shoe the same way someone with a narrow foot can.

Once I started thinking about it this way, every review I read felt incomplete. Because it was.

---

**SECTION 4: WHY THIS CHANNEL (2:30 - 3:30)**

**[Bryan walks to the baseline. Faces camera from a slight distance. More serious now.]**

I want to be honest about something.

I am not a professional athlete. I'm not a podiatrist. I'm not a biomechanics researcher. I'm a serious recreational hooper who decided to build a testing methodology and be rigorous about it.

What that means is: the data I give you is real, it's earned on the court, and it's not influenced by who sent me the shoe or how much the brand spent on their PR.

Right now — I buy every shoe I test. That matters. Because when I say STURDY, it's because the court said so. When I say NOT IT, it's because the shoe failed the test and I'm not going to tell you to spend your money on something that's going to let you down.

**[Bryan looks directly into camera. This is the core mission statement.]**

The Traction Report exists to answer one question:

*Will this shoe hold up when the game gets hard — for your specific game, your body, your court?*

Not "does it look good." Not "did a celebrity wear it in the Finals." Not "is it worth the hype."

Does it hold up. For *you*. When it counts.

---

**SECTION 5: WHAT TO EXPECT + CTA (3:30 - 4:15)**

**[Bryan's energy lifts. Forward momentum now — here's what's coming.]**

Here's what you're going to get on this channel:

**First Looks** — quick reactions when new shoes drop. No verdict yet. Just: here's what we're looking at.

**Traction Tests** — the flagship. Court testing, real conditions, real data. Traction on hardwood, traction on dusty floors, traction when your legs are tired.

**Containment Checks** — does the shoe actually hold your foot? Lateral cuts. Hard stops. The moments that decide games and ankles.

**Recovery Checks** — what happens to the shoe — and your body — after a long run. The thumb test. The foam retention data. The stuff nobody else is capturing.

**Still Hoopable?** — we take legacy shoes and run them through the full TTR system. Because sometimes the best shoe available was made ten years ago.

**And community content** — because the best data comes from real hoopers, and you're part of this lab.

**[Bryan picks up the basketball again. Relaxed. Confident.]**

Subscribe. Turn on notifications. Drop your archetype in the comments right now — I want to know who's in this community.

And if you've been burned by bad shoe advice before — this channel is for you.

We don't review sneakers. We lab them.

Let's get to work.

**[Bryan tosses the ball off camera. Cut to black. TTR logo appears with tagline: "We don't review sneakers. We lab them."]**

---

## SCRIPT 2: "We Tested 5 Basketball Shoes. Here's Who Each One Is ACTUALLY For."
### First Multi-Shoe Review Video | 8-10 Minute Equivalent

**Shoes:** Nike Air Zoom G.T. Cut 3 | Adidas Dame 8 | New Balance 1000v1 | Under Armour Curry 12 | Jordan Luka 3

---

**[VISUAL: Cold open. No intro. We're on the court already. Five pairs of shoes lined up on the baseline. Bryan walks into frame from the side, points at the shoes.]**

---

**COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:45)**

Five shoes. Five different verdicts. Five completely different players they're built for.

**[Bryan walks down the line, pointing at each shoe.]**

This one is for the player who lives and dies by their first step. This one is for the big man who needs his joints to survive the season. This one is for the hooper who's been playing for 20 years and still thinks he's the best player on the court. This one is for the player whose feet have been fighting narrow toebox designs their whole life. And this one? This one is the most versatile shoe we've tested this year — with one serious catch.

**[Bryan stops. Looks at camera.]**

We tested all five. On the court. In real conditions. For real minutes. Here's what we found — and more importantly, here's who each shoe is *actually* built for.

**[TITLE CARD: "We Tested 5 Basketball Shoes. Here's Who Each One Is Actually For." — THE TRACTION REPORT]**

---

**INTRO SECTION (0:45 - 1:30)**

**[Bryan at the camera, standing at half court]**

Quick context on how we test before we get into the results.

Every shoe goes through the same six-category matrix. Movement efficiency. Traction. Containment. Performance sustainability — meaning does it hold up through fatigue, not just fresh out the box. Recovery and joint protection. And the trust factor — would I actually wear this shoe in a game I cared about.

Then we map the results to the seven player archetypes. Because the same shoe is a different shoe depending on who's in it.

Let's start with the one that surprised me the most.

---

**SHOE 1: NIKE AIR ZOOM G.T. CUT 3 (1:30 - 3:00)**

**[Visual: Bryan laces up the GT Cut 3. Close up on the shoe. Then court footage begins.]**

**SPOKEN:**
The Nike Air Zoom G.T. Cut 3. Let's start with what the name tells you: this shoe was designed for cutting. Change of direction. Lateral quickness. Nike built this around the Shifty Guard.

**[Court footage: Bryan running lateral shuffles, change-of-direction drills, pull-up jumpers]**

**SPOKEN (voiceover):**
Traction — full herringbone. Bites immediately. On clean hardwood, this might be the sharpest bite I've felt this year. No warm-up period. The forefoot grip is instant.

**[Slow-mo of sole during lateral cut]**

**SPOKEN:**
Watch this. Full forefoot contact through the cut. No slide. That's the traction doing its job.

**[Cut to Bryan sitting, speaking directly to camera]**

**SPOKEN:**
Now here's where it gets interesting. Containment. The upper on the GT Cut 3 is — engineered. They've built lateral support directly into the structure of the upper. Not just padding. Actual architectural support.

What that means for a Shifty Guard: when you cut hard, the shoe pushes back against the force. Your foot doesn't migrate inside the shoe. The lateral cut you initiate is the lateral cut you complete. That connection is rare.

**[Brief court footage — hard cut left, hard cut right, freeze frame at point of maximum lateral pressure]**

**SPOKEN:**
Fatigue behavior — solid. At minute 60 the traction held. The cushion — Zoom Air in the forefoot — still had response. Not fresh out the box, but not dead either.

**[Bryan stands, holds the shoe up]**

**SPOKEN:**
Now. The catch.

This shoe is built for quickness. Low stack. Forefoot-biased cushion. If you're a Heavy Forward? You're going to feel every hard step in your joints by the third quarter. If you're an Older Hooper who needs sustained cushion protection? Look somewhere else.

This is a Shifty Guard shoe.

**VERDICT GRAPHIC: GT CUT 3**
- SHIFTY GUARD → STURDY
- EXPLOSIVE WING → STURDY
- HEAVY FORWARD → SHAKY
- OLDER HOOPER → NOT IT

---

**SHOE 2: ADIDAS DAME 8 (3:00 - 4:30)**

**[Visual: Bryan laces up Dame 8s. Court footage begins.]**

**SPOKEN:**
The Adidas Dame 8. Damian Lillard's signature. And I want to say upfront — Lillard is a Shifty Guard archetype playing at the highest level. So this shoe is engineered for that specific movement profile.

**[Court footage: Ballhandling, step-backs, quick lateral movements]**

**SPOKEN (voiceover):**
Traction — herringbone variant. Full coverage. Grips. Outdoors? No. Outdoor rubber compound is not this shoe's story. But on indoor hardwood — including dusty hardwood — it holds. I tested it on three different courts and the traction was consistent.

**[Bryan talking to camera, slightly more technical than GT Cut 3 section]**

**SPOKEN:**
Midfoot lockdown — this is the standout feature for me. Adidas built a cable-tensioning system through the midfoot that is not just marketing language. It actually works. When you pull the laces, the midfoot cinches. Your foot sits in the shoe. The lateral containment this creates is real.

Foot migration during cuts: minimal. I measured footprint position inside the shoe at rest versus under lateral load. Minimal shift. That's engineering, not luck.

**[Brief court footage — post-testing, Bryan removes shoe, does the thumb test]**

**SPOKEN:**
Cushion. Lightstrike Pro. Responsive foam — this is energy-return cushion, not plush comfort cushion. At minute 60, the thumb test showed good retention. The foam bounced back quickly. The cushion story here is efficiency, not luxury.

**[Bryan holds up shoe]**

**SPOKEN:**
Here's who the Dame 8 is and isn't for.

Shifty Guards and Explosive Wings — this is your shoe. The traction, the lockdown, the energy-return foam, the court feel. This is the complete package for your movement profile.

Wide Footers — the toebox runs narrow. Size up half a size minimum. Test it in store.

Heavy Forwards — the stack isn't there for serious downward load across a full game. Your joints need more than Lightstrike Pro under that kind of force.

**VERDICT GRAPHIC: DAME 8**
- SHIFTY GUARD → STURDY
- EXPLOSIVE WING → STURDY
- WIDE FOOTER → SHAKY (size up)
- HEAVY FORWARD → SHAKY
- OUTDOOR HOOPER → NOT IT

---

**SHOE 3: NEW BALANCE 1000V1 (4:30 - 5:45)**

**[Visual: Bryan laces up NB 1000s. Court footage immediately.]**

**SPOKEN:**
The New Balance 1000. This is the shoe I get the most questions about. Wide Footer community found this shoe and they love it. Let me tell you why — and where it falls short.

**[Court footage: Wide lateral cuts, forward drives, post movements]**

**SPOKEN (voiceover):**
First: the fit story. New Balance makes a legitimately wide toebox. If you're a Wide Footer who has been fighting narrow shoes your entire basketball career — the 1000 might be the first shoe that actually fits you correctly. The toebox volume is real.

**[Close-up of foot in shoe — flat, stable, not overhanging the midsole]**

**SPOKEN:**
See how the foot sits flat? No overhang on the midsole edge? For a Wide Footer, that baseline stability changes the entire shoe experience. Containment works differently when the fit is correct.

**[Bryan speaking to camera, more measured tone]**

**SPOKEN:**
Now the traction. Multidirectional pattern on the outsole. Grips well in straight lines and lateral movement. Not the sharpest bite in this test — the GT Cut 3 and Dame 8 both outperformed it on traction specifically. But solid. Consistent. No surprises.

The cushion stack — moderate. Not maximum protection, not minimal. Sits in the middle. For the Heavy Forward who needs serious joint protection, this isn't enough. For the Average Hooper or Shifty Guard who needs a comfortable wide-fitting shoe — this works.

**[Bryan holds shoe, more serious expression]**

**SPOKEN:**
Here's the containment warning — and this is important.

For narrow footers who are considering the 1000 because they heard good things: don't. The extra toebox room becomes a problem. Your foot migrates on lateral cuts. We tested this specifically and found measurable foot shift inside the shoe under lateral load for narrow-to-average feet. That's not good.

This shoe is built for Wide Footers. It's a Wide Footer shoe masquerading as a general basketball shoe.

**VERDICT GRAPHIC: NEW BALANCE 1000V1**
- WIDE FOOTER → STURDY
- HEAVY FORWARD → SHAKY
- NARROW/AVERAGE FOOTER → SHAKY to NOT IT
- OUTDOOR HOOPER → STURDY (durable outsole compound)

---

**SHOE 4: UNDER ARMOUR CURRY 12 (5:45 - 7:00)**

**[Visual: Bryan laces up Curry 12s. More clinical energy for this section.]**

**SPOKEN:**
Under Armour Curry 12. This is Stephen Curry's shoe. And Curry — despite being one of the greatest players of all time — plays like a Shifty Guard in an Explosive Wing's system. The shoe reflects that. But there are nuances here.

**[Court footage: Quick first steps, three-point pullups, hard lateral cuts, and notably — some second-game fatigue footage]**

**SPOKEN (voiceover):**
Traction. Wavy herringbone. Grips very well on clean hardwood. Outstanding actually. This was one of the stronger traction performances in this test. Outdoors — the rubber is softer than I'd like for outdoor durability, but it functions.

Containment. Low cut. This is a performance low-cut shoe. The ankle is on its own, which means the upper has to do the containment work. It does — for the first game. My concern emerged in the second game.

**[Bryan sitting, doing recovery check voiceover style]**

**SPOKEN:**
Recovery Check is where the Curry 12 shows its personality.

After one game? Outstanding. Warp cushion system performed. Traction held. Energy return was real. I walked off the court feeling like the shoe delivered.

Second game of the day? Different story. The lateral support under repeated load started to fatigue. Not failure — but reduction. For the high-volume hooper who plays back-to-back games, or the Older Hooper who's getting long minutes across multiple sessions per week — the Curry 12's support story starts to thin.

**[Bryan holds shoe up]**

**SPOKEN:**
This is a one-game shoe masquerading as a high-volume shoe. If you play one game, take recovery time, come back fresh? STURDY. If you're playing two games in a day or you're a high-volume hooper? Think carefully.

**VERDICT GRAPHIC: CURRY 12**
- SHIFTY GUARD (single game) → STURDY
- EXPLOSIVE WING (single game) → STURDY
- HIGH-VOLUME HOOPER → SHAKY
- OLDER HOOPER → SHAKY
- OUTDOOR HOOPER → SHAKY

**PRODUCTION NOTES:**

General:
- Film all five shoes in a single location — same court, consistent lighting
- Five different shooting sessions (ideally on different days for authentic fatigue data) OR one long session with documented timestamps
- Use a stationary wide shot for court footage AND a handheld closer shot for slow-mo detail

Shot list to plan for each shoe:
- Close-up lacing up
- Walking on court, first impression footage
- Lateral shuffles left and right (60fps for slow-mo)
- Hard stop drill (60fps)
- 45-degree cut (60fps)
- Straight-line sprint and stop
- Post movement (for Heavy Forward archetypes)
- Thumb test (after testing session)
- Bryan holding shoe talking to camera

Graphics to produce:
- TTR title card (consistent template)
- Verdict graphic for each shoe (STURDY/SHAKY/NOT IT by archetype)
- Final comparison summary card

Editing rhythm:
- Cold open: fast cuts, high energy
- Individual shoe sections: mix of court footage and direct camera
- Slow-mo on traction and containment moments
- Summary: steady, deliberate — let the information land
- Total runtime target: 9:30 - 10:30

Audio:
- Shoe sound on court (squeaking, impact) should be in the mix — it's authentic
- Low background music under court footage only — not during direct-to-camera segments
- Voiceover sections: cleaner audio, may need to re-record in post
