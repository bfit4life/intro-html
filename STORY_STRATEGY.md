# THE TRACTION REPORT — Instagram & TikTok Story Framework
## "You're seeing the lab before everyone else."

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

**Feed = Broadcast.**
Discovery. Reach. Authority. Searchability. Virality.
The feed is where the world finds you.

**Stories = Locker Room + Lab Access.**
Relationship. Loyalty. Inner-circle access. Behavioral conditioning. Audience identity.
Stories are where your real ones live.

Stories should feel like: *"You're seeing the lab before everyone else."*

NOT polished.
NOT overly produced.
NOT generic reposting.

If it looks like a brand deck, it's wrong.
If it looks like someone actually picked up a camera mid-test, it's right.

---

## THE ASYMMETRY THAT BUILDS LOYALTY

Feed rewards discovery.
Stories reward loyalty.

The audience that watches Stories gets what the feed audience never sees.
That asymmetry creates a VIP inner circle — people who self-select as the most engaged, most invested followers in the community.

You're not manufacturing loyalty. You're creating conditions for it.
The real ones will find Stories. And they'll stay.

## THE 6 STORY PILLARS

---

### PILLAR 1: LAB NOTES

**What it is:**
Raw insider observations that never make the feed. Immediate. Observational. Intelligent. Unfinished on purpose.

**Why it works:**
It's the thought before the thought. The note that doesn't make the review but is exactly what a real hooper wants to hear. It signals credibility through specificity. It makes the audience feel like they're inside the process — not watching the result.

**Voice:**
Direct. Present tense. No hedging. No intro. Like you just noticed something and grabbed your phone.

**Examples:**
- "This heel compression already feels softer after 2 runs."
- "Traction bites hard forward… but lateral stops are questionable."
- "Most reviewers won't notice this torsion issue."
- "The upper broke in faster than expected. That's a problem."
- "Court feel is different on this side of the gym. Running the same test on both ends."

**Cadence:** 3-5x per week when actively testing. Should feel like a stream of consciousness, not a scheduled post.

---

### PILLAR 2: BEFORE THE REVIEW

**What it is:**
Access before content drops. Pre-review footage that builds episodic momentum and conditions the audience to tune in when the full piece releases.

**Why it works:**
Creates a narrative arc. The audience invests in the process before seeing the verdict. When the full review drops, they feel like they already have context — and that context makes them more invested in the conclusion.

**Visual toolkit:**
- Outsole close-ups on hardwood
- Lace setup — flat, lock, standard
- Step-in feel reaction (first foot in, immediate response)
- Scale weight comparison
- Fit testing — width, toe box, heel lockdown
- Warm-up footage at speed
- Shoe squeak audio (raw, no music)
- Slow-motion lateral cuts
- Pre-run setup on the baseline

**Standard caption:**
"Full Traction Test drops [tomorrow / this week / Friday]."

**Cadence:** 2-3 Stories the day before a review drops. One the morning of.

---

### PILLAR 3: UNDER FATIGUE

**What it is:**
Post-run raw thoughts. Sweating. Breathing heavy. Honest reactions when the body is tired and the performance window is real.

**Why it works:**
This is the brand moat. Nobody owns this lane properly.
Fresh feel reviews are everywhere. Fatigue-state performance data is almost nowhere.
Cushion behaves differently after 40 minutes of full-court. Traction degrades. Lateral support fatigues.
Reviewers who only test fresh are giving you 15 minutes of data. TTR gives you the full run.

**The key insight:**
A shoe that feels incredible at minute 5 and collapses at minute 45 is a bad basketball shoe.
The audience needs to know that. The feed doesn't always have time for it.
Stories do.

**Voice:**
Tired but sharp. Not sloppy. Still intelligent. Just unfiltered.
The audience should hear the breathing. Feel the sweat. Believe it's real because it is.

**Examples:**
- "Legs are tired now. Cushion feels completely different than it did warming up."
- "Forefoot feels amazing fresh… not so much after an hour of full court."
- "That lateral stability I liked in the first 20 minutes? Gone."

**Cadence:** Every testing session. Post-run, before you even sit down.

---

### PILLAR 4: COURT INTELLIGENCE

**What it is:**
Quick educational teaching moments. 15-30 seconds. Simple camera. High trust.

**Why it works:**
Positions TTR as a knowledge authority, not just a reviewer. Makes the audience smarter. Smart audiences stay. They share. They trust. They come back.

**Topics:**
- Why traction patterns matter (herringbone vs. multidirectional vs. circular)
- Why heavy hoopers bottom out foam faster and what that means for shoe selection
- Why wide footers struggle in narrow bases and which shoes to avoid
- Why court feel changes movement behavior
- Why heel-to-toe drop affects how you cut
- Why outsole rubber durometer matters and nobody talks about it
- Why midsole stack height changes your center of gravity

**Voice:**
Smart but accessible. No jargon without explanation. ESPN commentator energy — you know the game, you're teaching the viewer, not showing off.

**Cadence:** 2x per week. Use as connective tissue between testing content.

---

### PILLAR 5: THE LOCKER ROOM

**What it is:**
Human content. Bryan Wilson exists here. Real moments between sessions.

**Why it works:**
The audience follows a person, not just a lab. The locker room is where they remember that Bryan actually hoops, actually recovers, actually has opinions about the game beyond footwear. It builds emotional connection without breaking brand identity.

**Content types:**
- Recovering after runs — ice, stretch, foam roll
- Organizing the shoe rotation
- Late-night editing — monitor glow, reviewing test footage
- NBA commentary — live reactions, post-game takes
- "What I'm testing next" reveals
- Honest moments: a shoe disappointing, a surprise performer, an off day
- The shoe that just got retired from the rotation

**Tone:**
Still Bryan. Still TTR. Just the version between takes.
No performance here. No content voice.
Just the hooper in the lab.

**Cadence:** 2-3x per week. Mix with heavier content.

---

### PILLAR 6: FOLLOWER ACCESS

**What it is:**
Identity-based engagement that makes the audience feel like participants in the lab, not just observers of it.

**Why it works:**
Generic engagement bait ("comment below!") performs poorly and attracts low-quality engagement. Identity-based engagement ("Which shoe failed you on a big night?") attracts real hoopers with real opinions. That's the TTR audience.

**The difference:**
- Bait: "Which shoe do you like better?"
- Identity: "Which shoe let you down when it mattered?"

The second one pulls real stories. Real hoopers who remember specific nights. That audience is more valuable, more loyal, and more likely to share.

**Formats:**
- Polls: "Which shoe failed you?" / "Best traction this year?" / "Which setup aged badly?"
- A/B comparisons: Two outsoles. One question. Vote now.
- Sliders: Rate the traction. Rate the cushion. Rate the value.
- "Which one enters the lab next?" — community-driven test queue
- DM-based: "Tell me your worst court shoe experience. I read every one."

**Cadence:** 2x per week. Always tied to active testing or upcoming content.

## VISUAL STYLE FOR STORIES

**Color palette:**
Dark charcoal backgrounds. Hardwood gold accents. Court concrete greys. Orange neon highlights for key text or alerts.

**Camera approach:**
- Handheld, POV, slightly imperfect framing — not shaky, but alive
- Close-up traction shots — outsole on hardwood, rubber texture, wear patterns
- Slow motion lateral cuts — 240fps or 120fps for key moments
- Breathing after runs — camera stays on the face, no cut
- Squeak audio — raw, unprocessed, real court sound
- Gym lighting — real gym, real conditions, not a studio

**Typography overlays:**
- Clean, minimal
- High contrast (white or gold on dark)
- Short bursts — 3-5 words max per card
- No script fonts. Bold sans-serif only.

**The feel:**
Immediate. Alive. Unfiltered. Credible.
If it looks like it took more than 10 minutes to produce, it's too much.
The rawness is the brand signal.
