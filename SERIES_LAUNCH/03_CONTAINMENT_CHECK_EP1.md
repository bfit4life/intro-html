# CONTAINMENT CHECK — EPISODE 1
## Shoe: Nike KD 4

**Series:** Containment Check
**Episode:** 001
**Target Length:** 35–50 seconds
**Format:** Technical. Movement-focused. Controversial angle. Real hooper differentiator.

**Why this shoe:**
The KD 4 is a legend. One of the most celebrated basketball shoes ever made. Kevin Durant wore it to one of the most iconic stretches of individual performance in NBA history. But the KD 4 is narrow. Low-cut. Built for a 6'10" forward with a guard's skill set and a relatively narrow foot profile. The question is real: can this shoe actually contain an explosive wing who isn't built like Durant? That tension is the episode.

**Controversial angle:**
"Everyone says the KD 4 is a guard shoe. But can it actually contain an explosive wing?"

---

## FULL PRODUCTION SCRIPT

---

**[HOOK — Provocative. Drop the question before they can settle in.]*

*[Shot: KD 4 on court. Bryan's feet. Quick lateral shuffle. Then stop. Direct to camera.]*

**Bryan:**
"The KD 4 is one of the greatest basketball shoes ever made.

But let me ask you something nobody asks:

Can it actually contain your foot?

Or is the legend getting in the way of the truth?"

---

**[LATERAL STABILITY SEQUENCE — The test.]*

*[Bryan moves on court. Lateral shuffles, defensive slides, hard direction changes.]*

**Bryan (V.O.):**
"The Containment Check is about one thing:
when you plant, cut, and change direction —
does the shoe hold your foot in place?
Or does your foot move inside the shoe
while the shoe stays on the floor?

Because those are two different problems.
And most people can't tell them apart until it's too late.

The KD 4 is a low-cut shoe. Very low.
And it's narrow through the midfoot.
That combination means two things:
one, your ankle is exposed.
Two, wider feet are going to feel the walls."

---

**[LATERAL STABILITY SPECIFIC FINDING — What Bryan actually observed.]*

**Bryan (V.O.):**
"On lateral cuts — the shoe performs reasonably.
The outsole is sticky enough that the floor contact is solid.
It's not sliding out from under you.

But here's where it gets interesting.

The heel. Watch the heel."

*[Slow-mo shot: heel during lateral cut. You can see the heel slightly lifting off the footbed.]*

**Bryan (V.O.):**
"There's heel lift happening on sharp direction changes.
The heel counter on the KD 4 is not deep.
It's not built to lock a heel down hard.
It was built to be light.

And at 6'10" and 240 pounds — Durant's heel wasn't torquing the way an explosive 220-pound wing's heel torques on a hard cut."

---

**[HEEL LOCKDOWN TEST — Key performance finding.]*

**Bryan:**
"Heel lockdown test.
I'm pressing my foot back into the collar, loading the heel,
and cutting hard to my left.

The heel moves.
Not dramatically. But it moves.
And when your heel moves inside a shoe on a hard cut —
your ankle is compensating for that instability.

Over the course of a game, that adds up."

---

**[FOOT MOVEMENT UNDER FATIGUE — The deeper issue.]*

**Bryan (V.O.):**
"Forty minutes in — this is where containment reveals itself.
When your foot is fatigued, it swells slightly.
The muscles around your ankle are working harder to stabilize.

A shoe with strong containment holds you up when your body gets tired.
A shoe with weak containment starts working against you.

The KD 4, deep in a session, becomes a liability for certain players."

---

**[VERDICT — Who can trust it. Who cannot.]*

*[Bryan, direct to camera. Settled. The verdict is measured, not dramatic.]*

**Bryan:**
"Here's the verdict.

The KD 4 containment check — SHAKY.

Not NOT IT. SHAKY.

If you have a narrow foot, if you're a true guard —
a point guard, a floor general — under 200 pounds —
this shoe can work for you.
The containment is adequate for your game.

But if you're a wing. An explosive wing.
If you're 210, 220, you're driving hard left and right,
you're a slasher who puts weight into your cuts —

this shoe was not built for your heel.
It was not built for your width.
It was not built to contain the force you generate.

The legend is real.
The containment has limits.
Know which side of the line you're on."

**Bryan (V.O.):**
"Containment Check. Every TTR episode.
Because knowing is what keeps you on the court."

## VISUAL DIRECTION

**Shot 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:05):**
- KD 4 on hardwood — tight shot. Bryan's feet moving laterally. Then stop.
- Bryan looks directly into camera. Confident, slightly challenging.
- Hook text overlay: **"Can the KD 4 actually contain your foot?"**

**Shot 2 — LATERAL STABILITY SEQUENCE (0:06–0:18):**
- Side angle, waist-down: Bryan doing lateral defensive slides in the KD 4
- Camera at ankle height — show the shoe at floor level during the movement
- Cut to: front angle, showing the medial wall of the shoe under pressure during a cut
- Slow motion replay of one sharp lateral cut — full 50% slow-mo, then real time

**Shot 3 — HEEL LIFT CLOSE-UP (0:18–0:26):**
- This is the money shot. Camera positioned at heel height, behind and to the side.
- Bryan plants and cuts. Slow-mo on the heel during the cut.
- The heel lift should be visible — even if subtle. If not visible on camera, supplement with Bryan demonstrating by hand on the shoe.
- Data overlay: **HEEL LIFT DETECTED ⚠**

**Shot 4 — HEEL LOCKDOWN TEST (0:26–0:32):**
- Bryan demonstrates the heel press test — foot in shoe, pressing back, then cutting.
- Top-down optional: looking straight down at the heel counter from above
- Bryan's hands showing the heel counter depth — shallow vs. deep comparison (can hold up a different shoe for reference)

**Shot 5 — FOOT MOVEMENT UNDER FATIGUE (0:32–0:38):**
- Quick shots: late in a session — Bryan's movements, slightly labored
- If possible, side-by-side or back-to-back: early session vs. late session lateral cut
- Annotation overlay: foot movement inside the shoe, fatigue phase

**Shot 6 — VERDICT (0:40–0:50):**
- Bryan, direct camera. Static. Let each verdict line breathe.
- Graphic: **SHAKY ⚠** — amber treatment
- Archetype breakdown graphic: who it works for vs. who it doesn't
  - WORKS FOR: Narrow foot / True guard / Under 200 lbs
  - NOT FOR: Explosive wing / Heavy guard / Wide foot

**Overall aesthetic:** This is a technical episode. The visual language should feel like sport science — angles that show the foot, the heel, the interface between shoe and court. Slow-motion is essential. Less Bryan talking, more Bryan moving and the camera catching what the eye misses in real time.
