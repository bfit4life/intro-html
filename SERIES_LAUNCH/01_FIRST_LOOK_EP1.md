# FIRST LOOK — EPISODE 1
## Shoe: New Balance OG-1 (Shai OG-1)

**Series:** First Look
**Episode:** 001
**Target Length:** 30–45 seconds
**Format:** Visual-heavy. Curiosity driver. Short and punchy.

**Why this shoe for launch:**
New Balance is back in basketball — for real this time. The OG-1 is a statement shoe. Not everyone in the casual audience knows it exists yet. That's the hook. Curiosity plus credibility. And Shai Gilgeous-Alexander wearing it gives it mainstream pull without making it a sneakerhead shoe — it's a performance story first.

## FULL PRODUCTION SCRIPT

---

**[HOOK — First 2 seconds. No intro. Straight to it.]**

*[Shot: Extreme close-up of the OG-1 outsole — the traction pattern filling the entire frame. Held for 1 second. Then Bryan's voice.]*

**Bryan (V.O.):**

And they are not playing around."

---

**[VISUAL WALKTHROUGH — Shoe in hand, Bryan examining it on camera.]*

**Bryan:**
"This is the OG-1. Shai's shoe.
And the first thing you notice — this is not a retro play.
This is not New Balance trying to be nostalgic.
This is a brand saying: we want to build the best basketball shoe in the world.

Look at this shape — low to the ground, wide base.
Stability platform built into the foundation.
Not afterthought stability — structural stability.

The upper is a woven mesh. Light, but there's structure here.
It's not going to fold on you.

Flip it over — the outsole. Herringbone pattern.
Full coverage, no cutouts.
That tells me they're prioritizing court grip over weight savings.
I respect that choice."

---

**[STEP-IN FEEL — Brief, sensory description.]*

**Bryan:**
"First step-in?
Surprisingly snug without being tight.
There's a slight heel cup that grabs you — like the shoe knows where your foot is.
Cushioning feels responsive. Not plush. Responsive.
This shoe wants to move."

---

**[WHO IS THIS FOR — The archetype setup.]*

**Bryan:**
"So who is this actually built for?

This is a guard shoe. Floor General. Slasher.
Somebody who needs a low, stable platform
and wants traction they can trust in traffic.

But here's the question I have —
and it's the question this whole channel exists to answer:

Does it hold up under fatigue?
Does the traction survive a dusty court?
Does the containment actually work at game speed?

That's what the lab is for.
Full traction test — coming soon.
Don't sleep on this one."

---

**[CLOSE — TTR sign-off.]*

*[Quick cut to TTR logo or text slate.]*

## VISUAL DIRECTION

**Shot 1 — THE HOOK (0:00–0:03):**
- Extreme macro close-up of outsole. Herringbone pattern. Fill the frame.
- Camera slowly pulls back to reveal the full shoe.
- No text. No Bryan yet. Let the shoe introduce itself.

**Shot 2 — SHAPE AND STRUCTURE (0:04–0:15):**
- Bryan in frame, shoe in hand. Turning it over. Deliberate, not flashy.
- Camera angles: profile of the shoe, bottom-up outsole shot, side wall shot showing the stability platform
- Lighting: Clean, even. Shoe lit separately — not just ambient gym light. Treat the shoe like a subject.
- No fancy cinematography. Just clear, honest product shots.

**Shot 3 — UPPER DETAIL (0:12–0:18):**
- Tight shot of the woven mesh upper. Bryan's thumb pressing on it to show flexibility vs. structure.
- Cut to: heel counter — show the cup from behind.

**Shot 4 — STEP-IN FEEL (0:20–0:26):**
- Bryan lacing up or slipping foot in — show the heel grab from the side.
- Optional: quick overhead shot of foot inside shoe, showing fit.

**Shot 5 — ARCHETYPE SETUP (0:28–0:38):**
- Back to Bryan, direct to camera. Simple background.
- Confident. Teasing. Not rushing.

**Shot 6 — CLOSE + LOGO (0:40–0:45):**
- Quick TTR logo card. Clean. 2 seconds max.

**Overall aesthetic:** Clean product shots mixed with Bryan's real-world evaluation style. Not studio. Not sneakerhead photography. Purposeful.

## AUDIO DIRECTION

- **Hook (0:00–0:03):** Silence. Then Bryan's voice drops in cold.
- **Mid-video:** Light trap/percussion beat — subtle, under the voice, not competing with it. Think: focused energy, not hype.
- **Beat accent** on "And they are not playing around" — brief accent, then back to steady pulse.
- **Step-in feel section:** Pull the music back slightly — make it feel more intimate, observational.
- **Archetype + close:** Beat can come back up slightly as the tease builds.
- **Outro:** Beat fades with logo.

**Voice treatment:** Minimal processing. Bryan sounds like Bryan. Clear, present, direct.
