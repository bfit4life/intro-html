🛹 Slant Board Training — The Science

Why Slant Board? A 15–25° heel-elevated board increases ankle dorsiflexion, shifts load to the quadriceps, allows deeper knee flexion without lumbar stress, and directly trains the elastic tendon system basketball jumpers rely on.

Quad Activation: Research shows slant board squats activate the VMO (vastus medialis) 20–30% more than flat squats — critical for knee stability, patellar tracking, and jump power.

Tendon Protocol: Isometrics at 70–90° knee angle reduce patellar tendon pain by 40%+ immediately (Rio et al.). 5×45 sec at 4/10 max pain. This is your tendon armor.

For a 42-Year-Old: Collagen synthesis slows with age. Slow, loaded tendon work (isometrics + eccentrics) on the slant board is the most evidence-based approach to keeping knees explosive AND pain-free through your 50s.

Progressions: BW squats → Goblet → Barbell front load → Single-leg. Always lead with movement quality over load.

Key Rule: Isometrics LAST in the session (post-fatigue tendon loading). Plyometrics FIRST when CNS is fresh.

🔄 Longevity Architecture

Recovery System

Active recovery · Soft tissue · Nervous system management · Deload protocol

Thursday — Active Recovery Day Protocol

Morning (30–45 min)
  • 10 min morning sun walk (barefoot if possible)
  • Full-body mobility flow — hip/ankle/thoracic
  • Slant board mobility (NOT strength work)
  • Light tibialis + soleus activation (1×15 each)
  • Foot self-massage with lacrosse ball (5 min)
Evening (20–30 min)
  • 20–30 min zone 2 walk (2–2.5 mph — talking pace)
  • Target 8,000–10,000 steps total on recovery day
  • Foam roll full lower body (calves, quads, IT, glutes)
  • Light yoga or hip stretching routine
  • Contrast shower (3 min hot / 1 min cold × 3)

Nervous System Management
  • Limit max-effort sessions to 4/week
  • HRV monitoring — if <baseline, reduce load
  • No high-intensity 2 days consecutive
  • Deload when 2+ joints aching together
  • Mental stress = physical recovery cost
